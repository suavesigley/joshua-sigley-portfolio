SECURITY WRITING

Starting My Security Writing Journey

Documenting the transition into cybersecurity, security operations and practical investigation.

I’m Joshua Sigley, an early-career cybersecurity practitioner focused on security operations, threat detection and incident investigation.

I’m currently working in concrete while deliberately building the skills and portfolio needed to transition into IT and cybersecurity. I completed two years of a Bachelor of Cyber Security and hold the ISC2 Certified in Cybersecurity (CC). I’m currently studying for CompTIA Security+ and sit in the top 3% on TryHackMe. Alongside formal study, I’ve been developing practical capability through structured labs, simulated investigations and hands-on infrastructure work.

My interest in technology started long before any formal course. Growing up as the eldest sibling with parents who weren’t particularly tech-savvy, troubleshooting became my responsibility from a young age. Whether it was fixing a slow computer, setting up devices, or figuring out why something stopped working, I was the person the household turned to. That early problem-solving habit never really left — I’ve always been the one tinkering with technology.

Over the past year, that curiosity has become more focused. Rather than only collecting certificates, I’ve been documenting the process: the investigations, the mistakes, the lessons and the workflows. This writing space is where I’ll continue that documentation as I work toward junior SOC analyst and security operations roles.

What You Can Expect

  • Investigation write-ups based on simulated security incidents
  • Lessons from building and running my home lab, Project Hydra
  • Connections between IT support work and security operations
  • Notes on security processes, methodology and what I’m currently learning
  • Practical documentation of the tools and techniques I use

You can also explore a full example of my investigation approach in the SOC Analyst Investigation case study.

Everything published here is lab-based or simulated. I will always be clear about the scope and limitations of the work. These posts are not presented as professional client experience or production incident response.

Why I’m Writing

I learn best when I force myself to explain what I did, why I did it and what I would improve next time.

Writing is also a way to create clear evidence of how I approach problems — something that matters when applying for junior security operations roles.

The focus will stay practical. You’ll see structured investigation workflows, Windows event analysis, MITRE ATT&CK mapping, ticket-style documentation and the small technical decisions that sit behind a finished lab or report.

How I’ll Approach It

I prefer clarity over complexity.

Posts will prioritise evidence, methodology and honest reflection rather than polished “success only” stories. When something breaks or takes longer than expected, that will be part of the write-up.

This series will document both the technical work and the transition itself — the labs, the investigations and the process of moving from a trade background into security operations.

If you’re also early in your cybersecurity journey, balancing work with study, or simply interested in how junior practitioners approach security investigations and documentation, you’re welcome to follow along.

More posts will follow as I continue building and investigating.

— Joshua Sigley
